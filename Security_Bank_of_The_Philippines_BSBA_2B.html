Background & History

Security Bank Corporation (commonly known as Security Bank) is one of the leading universal banks in the Philippines. It was established on June 18, 1951, as Security Bank and Trust Company (SBTC), making it the first private, Filipino-controlled bank of the post-World War II era. Its primary mission at that time was to provide financial services that would help in rebuilding the country after the war.

Throughout its history, Security Bank has consistently expanded its services, introducing various innovations in the banking sector. In 1994, it changed its name to Security Bank Corporation to reflect its diversification into a full-service universal banking institution. The bank has earned multiple awards for its excellence in consumer banking, corporate banking, and treasury operations.

Today, Security Bank offers a wide range of services, including retail, commercial, corporate banking, and trust and asset management services. It is known for its brand promise, "BetterBanking," which focuses on customer-centric products and personalized service.

Corporate Profile

Security Bank Corporation is a publicly listed universal bank in the Philippines, offering a comprehensive range of financial products and services across retail, corporate, commercial, and institutional markets. Founded in 1951, Security Bank has established itself as one of the country's most stable and respected banks, known for its commitment to innovation, customer service, and operational excellence.

The bank operates through various business segments including consumer banking, commercial banking, corporate and investment banking, and asset management. Security Bank also engages in treasury operations, foreign exchange trading, and securities services.

Strategic Partnership

Security Bank is known for its strategic partnership with MUFG Bank, Japan's largest bank, which acquired a 20% stake in Security Bank in 2016. This alliance strengthens the bank's capabilities in providing world-class financial services and in supporting the cross-border business expansion of its clients.

Subsidiaries and Affiliates

  • SB Cards Corporation – Provides credit card services as a joint venture with The Bank of Tokyo-Mitsubishi UFJ, Ltd. (now MUFG Bank).
  • SB Finance Company, Inc. – A partnership with Thailand's Bank of Ayudhya (Krungsri), offering personal loans, car loans, and other financing services.
  • Security Bank Savings, Inc. – A thrift bank focused on serving the mass retail market with a range of deposit and loan products.
  • SB Equities, Inc. – Handles the bank's brokerage business and is a member of the Philippine Stock Exchange.
  • SB Capital Investment Corporation – Engages in investment banking services, including debt and equity underwriting, mergers and acquisitions, and advisory services.

Scope of Business/Operation

Security Bank is an independent bank with core businesses in Wholesale Banking, Retail Banking, and Financial Markets, with a solid niche among the mass affluent Filipino-Chinese community. The advantage of being an independent bank is that customers get comfort in the fact that Security Bank does not compete with any of their businesses.

The Bank's three core businesses are managed separately to focus on their respective markets and they operate with a high degree of synergy among each other.

Core Business Areas

  • Wholesale Banking comprises two groups: Corporate & Investment Banking, which focuses on the top 1,000 corporates, institutional, and public sector markets; and Commercial Middle Market Banking which focuses on the middle markets and small-and-medium enterprises.
  • Retail Banking focuses on the individual and retail markets, providing deposit-taking and servicing, mortgage, auto, personal loans and small business loans, and credit card facilities.
  • Financial Markets focuses on providing money market, foreign exchange, financial derivatives, securities distribution, asset management, trust, and fiduciary services.

Security Bank is driven by superior customer service and profitability first and foremost, not by size or market share. Security Bank will pursue build-up in market share only if it increases profitability and shareholder value.

Market Position

Security Bank is the 8th largest in total assets among private domestic universal banks in the country, with total assets of Php 653Bn; 7th largest in loans, with a loan portfolio of Php 439Bn; and 5th largest in capital, with capital of Php 123Bn as of December 31, 2020.

The Bank is known among investors for its superior financial results. Prior to the strategic partnership and capital investment by MUFG in 2016, in particular during the years 2007-2012, and 2014 and 2015, Security Bank ranked 1st in return on shareholders' equity (ROE), 1st in return on assets (ROA), 1st in cost-to-income ratio, 1st in asset quality (having the lowest non-performing loans ratio) and 1st in NPL reserve cover ratio, among private domestic universal banks.

Strategic Partnership with MUFG

It is this track record of superior results that attracted Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group, Inc. (MUFG) to invest Php37 billion in equity capital for a 20% stake in Security Bank in 2016. To date, this is the largest equity investment in a Philippine financial institution by a foreign investor. Security Bank is elated to work with MUFG because the two banks share the same values of integrity, professionalism, and teamwork.

Business Strategy

Security Bank has invested and continues to invest in the expansion of its retail banking business and distribution channels (i.e., branch network, digital channels, and alternative channels) to drive future revenues. The Bank's strategy, execution, and results are guided by its strong focus on customer relationships, service quality, and shareholder value.

BetterBanking Commitment

Security Bank found from its market research and focused group discussions several years ago that there was a clear need in the customer market for better-quality banking services. Customers were not happy with the quality of service from their banks. Thus, the BetterBanking commitment was born.

BetterBanking is about knowing in-depth the customers' needs and circumstances, and then servicing them and providing solutions in a professional, problem-free, personalized, and prompt manner — the four P's of BetterBanking. The Bank is working to consistently deliver to customers the BetterBanking experience in any branch and access channel they use.

Now, Security Bank customers enjoy enhancements in services including faster and paperless branch transactions, priority banking, and digital banking. These have benefited both the older-generation customers who still like to do face-to-face banking with the Bank and the younger-generation customers who like to do things online.

The quality of Security Bank's services and results is shown in the awards it has received, highlighted by Bank of the Year-Philippines awards from The Banker of London in four of the last eight years (2018, 2016, 2015, and 2012). In addition, Security Bank was also awarded The Best Retail Bank in the Philippines for 2018 and 2019 by The Asian Banker.

  • SB Equities, a stock brokerage company, one of the top domestic brokerages in the country.

Security Bank's Board of Directors is responsible for the overall strategic direction and oversight of the bank. The board members are elected by the shareholders and are responsible for making key decisions about the bank's policies, strategies, and operations.

Board Composition

Frederick Y. Dy

Chairman Emeritus

Frederick Y. Dy, 69, Filipino, was elected Chairman Emeritus on April 28, 2015. He was elected Vice Chairman of the Board on April 4, 1989 before assuming the Chairman's position from April 1991 to April 2015.

Cirilo P. Noel

Chairman

Cirilo P. Noel, 67, Filipino, was elected as Chairman of the Board of Security Bank Corporation on May 7, 2024. Prior to this, Mr. Noel was Interim Chairman from May 3, 2023 to May 6, 2024, and was Vice Chairman of the Board from April 28, 2020 to May 2, 2023.

Management Committee

The Management Committee is responsible for the day-to-day operations of Security Bank, implementing the strategies and policies set by the Board of Directors, and ensuring that the bank operates efficiently and effectively.

Board Committees

To oversee the implementation and execution of the bank's strategies, Security Bank Corporation has 11 Board committees:

Executive Committee

Oversees and coordinates the bank's day-to-day operations and strategic initiatives.

Audit Committee

Responsible for overseeing the bank's financial reporting process, internal controls, and audit functions.

Risk Oversight Committee

Reviews and assesses the adequacy of the bank's risk management framework and policies.

Corporate Governance Committee

Ensures the bank's compliance with good corporate governance principles and practices.

Trust Committee

Oversees the bank's trust and fiduciary operations and ensures compliance with applicable regulations.

Related Party Transactions Committee

Reviews and evaluates transactions between the bank and its related parties to ensure fairness and transparency.

Senior Credit Committee

Reviews and approves credit proposals, credit policies, and procedures.

Nominations and Remuneration Committee

Reviews and evaluates qualifications of candidates nominated to the Board and senior management positions.

Transformation and Technology Committee

Oversees the bank's digital transformation initiatives and technology infrastructure.

Information Security Risk Management Committee

A sub-committee of the Risk Oversight Committee that oversees the bank's information security framework and policies.

For more detailed information about Security Bank's corporate governance and committee structures, visit the official Corporate Governance page on the Security Bank website.

Capital Structure

As of the end of 2024, Security Bank Corporation maintained a robust capital structure and met regulatory capital adequacy requirements. Below is a summary of its capital metrics and liquidity ratios:

Capital Structure & Adequacy (as of December 31, 2024)

Capital Ratios

Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Ratio: 12.9%

Total Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR): 13.8%

Shareholders' Equity: ₱141.14 billion, reflecting a 3.68% increase from the previous year

Liquidity Ratios

Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR): 178%

Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR): 130%

These figures indicate that Security Bank maintains capital levels well above the minimum regulatory requirements set by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), ensuring financial stability and resilience.

Trends and Observations

  • The CET1 Ratio decreased from 15.3% in 2023 to 12.9% in 2024, and the CAR declined from 16.19% to 13.8% over the same period. This reduction is attributed to significant loan growth and asset expansion during the year.
  • Despite the decrease, both ratios remain comfortably above the BSP's minimum requirements, indicating a solid capital position.
  • The bank's liquidity ratios, LCR and NSFR, are well above the regulatory thresholds, reflecting prudent liquidity management.

Governance Framework

Security Bank's corporate governance framework is designed to ensure responsible leadership, ethical business conduct, and sustainable growth. The framework encompasses:

  • Board Oversight: A competent and diverse Board of Directors that provides strategic guidance and monitors management performance.
  • Risk Management: Comprehensive systems to identify, measure, and manage risks across all levels of the organization.
  • Transparency: Timely and accurate disclosure of material information to all stakeholders.
  • Accountability: Clear definition of roles and responsibilities throughout the organization.

Corporate Social Responsibility

Security Bank Corporation, through its Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) arm, Security Bank Foundation, Inc. (SBFI), is committed to making a positive impact on communities across the Philippines. The bank focuses its CSR efforts on education, environmental sustainability, and community development.

Notable CSR Initiatives

Build a School, Build a Nation: The Classrooms Project

In 2024, SBFI continued its flagship program by donating multiple school buildings across various regions in the Philippines. Notably, they reached their 800th classroom milestone with a new building donation in Pagadian City.

This initiative addresses the classroom shortage in public schools and provides conducive learning environments for Filipino students.

Regalo Mo, Kinabukasan Ko (RMKK) Scholarship Program

Since its inception in 2010, the RMKK scholarship program has supported over 2,600 scholars across various educational levels. In 2024, the program continued to empower deserving students, exemplifying the bank's commitment to education.

The program provides financial assistance to academically gifted but economically challenged students, helping them achieve their educational goals.

La Mesa Watershed Reforestation Project

Security Bank, in partnership with ABS-CBN Foundation's Bantay Kalikasan program, is two years into a five-year commitment to plant 10,000 trees in the La Mesa Watershed. This initiative aims to restore the ecosystem and promote environmental sustainability.

The La Mesa Watershed is a critical source of water for Metro Manila, and this reforestation effort helps protect this vital natural resource.

Food Shed Farming Enterprise Project

On July 6, 2024, Security Bank collaborated with the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF) Philippines to launch the Food Shed Farming Enterprise Project in Barangay Hilamonan, Kabankalan City, Negros Occidental. The project addresses food and nutrition security by establishing community-managed food sheds.

This initiative empowers local communities to produce their own food sustainably, enhancing food security and promoting agricultural entrepreneurship.

Vision Care Program for Students

In partnership with Ideal Vision Center, SBFI provided free vision screenings and eyeglasses to students in various schools, including those in San Pablo and San Pedro, Laguna. This initiative aims to improve students' learning experiences by addressing vision problems.

By addressing vision issues that may hinder academic performance, this program helps ensure that students can fully participate in and benefit from their education.
